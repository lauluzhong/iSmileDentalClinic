## The Main Types of Dentures

Each option solves a slightly different problem.

- **Partial denture:** a removable plate that replaces several missing teeth and sits alongside the natural teeth you still have.
- **Complete denture:** a removable set that replaces all the teeth in the upper or lower jaw.
- **Implant-retained full-arch denture:** a full set of teeth supported by dental implants placed in the jaw, so the denture is held by the implants rather than resting on the gums alone.
- **Dental bridge:** not a denture, but often discussed at the same time. A bridge is a fixed restoration that fills a gap left by one or more missing teeth.

Many people come in asking about one option and leave having talked through two or three. That is normal. The aim is to match the replacement to the mouth, not the other way round.

## Signs It May Be Time to Talk About Dentures

<ul style="list-style:none;padding-left:0">
<li>✅ Several teeth are missing and chewing has become harder</li>
<li>✅ You avoid certain foods because they are difficult to bite or chew</li>
<li>✅ Gaps are affecting how comfortable you feel when you smile or speak</li>
<li>✅ An existing denture feels loose, rubs, or has cracked</li>
<li>✅ A parent has quietly switched to softer food and you are not sure why</li>
</ul>

None of these are emergencies. They are simply good reasons to have a look and talk through the options.

## What the Process Usually Involves

Dentures are made to fit one person's mouth, so the process takes a few visits. The exact steps depend on the type of denture and what the dentist finds, but a typical path looks like this.

1. **Examination and history.** The dentist checks the remaining teeth, gums and any existing denture, and asks about your general health and medications.
2. **Imaging where it helps.** X-rays show what is happening below the gum line. If implants are being considered, a 3D CBCT scan can show the bone in detail, using lower radiation than a medical CT.
3. **Talking through options.** You hear what is suitable for your mouth, what each option involves, and how many visits to expect.
4. **Preparing the mouth.** Sometimes a tooth needs treatment or removal first, or, for implant-retained dentures, the bone may need grafting.
5. **Records and try-in.** The shape of your mouth is recorded, and you may try the denture before it is finished so the bite and appearance can be checked.
6. **Fitting.** The completed denture is fitted, and you are shown how to put it in, take it out and clean it.
7. **Adjustments.** It is common to come back once or twice so any sore spots can be eased.

For implant-retained dentures, the implant stage adds healing time. Your dentist will advise on timing for your case.

## What Families Often Find Hardest

The clinical side is usually the easy part to explain. What wears people down is everything around it.

<ul style="list-style:none;padding-left:0">
<li>➖ <strong>Several visits.</strong> A new denture rarely happens in one appointment, and adjustments add more.</li>
<li>➖ <strong>Getting there.</strong> For an older parent, stairs, parking and long waits can matter as much as the treatment.</li>
<li>➖ <strong>Fit changes over time.</strong> Gums and bone can change shape, so a denture that fitted well years ago may now feel loose.</li>
<li>➖ <strong>Too many decisions at once.</strong> Partial or complete, removable or implant-supported, repair or replace.</li>
</ul>

Adult children often handle the planning, taking time off work to drive a parent across town. Fewer trips and clear answers at each visit make a real difference.

## Caring for Dentures at Home

A few everyday habits help dentures stay comfortable.

<ul style="list-style:none;padding-left:0">
<li>✅ Clean the denture daily with a soft brush, as the dentist shows you</li>
<li>✅ Keep brushing any natural teeth and gently clean the gums</li>
<li>✅ Handle the denture over a folded towel or a basin of water, in case it slips</li>
<li>✅ Come back if anything rubs, clicks or feels loose, rather than putting up with it</li>
<li>✅ Keep up regular check-ups, even with a full set of dentures</li>
</ul>

(A small tip from many families: keep a spare denture case in the car or handbag. It saves a lot of wrapping in tissue at restaurants.)
