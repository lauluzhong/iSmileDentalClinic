A dental implant, bridge and denture can all replace missing teeth, but they work in different ways. An implant is fixed in the jaw, a bridge is fixed to neighbouring teeth, and a conventional denture is removable.

The option that fits your needs depends on the gap, the nearby teeth, the available bone, your health and how you feel about surgery, cleaning and a removable appliance.

## Comparison at a glance

| Question | Dental implant | Dental bridge | Conventional denture |
|---|---|---|---|
| How is it held? | A titanium implant is placed in the jaw and supports a crown | The teeth beside the gap support a fixed replacement tooth | The denture rests on the gums and is removed by the patient |
| Is implant surgery involved? | Yes | No implant placement | No, unless implants are used to retain the denture |
| What happens to neighbouring teeth? | They usually do not need to support the implant | Support teeth are usually reshaped for crowns | They are not prepared as bridge supports |
| When might it be discussed? | One tooth, several teeth, or support for a larger restoration | A gap beside teeth suitable for supporting a bridge | Several missing teeth, teeth missing in different areas, or a complete arch |
| Daily care | Brush and clean around the implant crown | Brush and clean under the bridge | Remove and clean it as instructed |

This table is a starting point, not a diagnosis. A dentist still needs to examine the gums, remaining teeth, bite and bone before advising which routes are suitable.

## How each treatment works

### Dental implant

A dental implant replaces the missing root with a titanium implant placed in the jaw. After the area heals, a crown or another dental restoration is connected to it.

Implant treatment involves surgery and usually has more stages than a bridge or conventional denture. The amount and shape of bone matter because the implant needs support. A 3D CBCT scan can help the dentist assess the bone and plan placement. If there is not enough bone, bone grafting may be one of the options discussed.

An implant can replace a single tooth without using the teeth beside the gap as supports. Implants can also support replacements for several teeth or help retain a full-arch denture.

### Dental bridge

A bridge is fixed in the mouth and fills a gap with a replacement tooth connected to crowns on the supporting teeth. Those neighbouring teeth are usually reshaped so the crowns can fit over them.

That trade-off matters. If the teeth beside the gap are healthy and intact, you may prefer an option that does not involve preparing them. If those teeth already have large fillings or crowns and need restoration, a bridge may be a sensible route to discuss.

A bridge does not involve placing an implant in the jaw. It still needs careful cleaning, including under the replacement tooth, because plaque can collect around the supports.

### Denture

A conventional denture is removable. A partial denture replaces some missing teeth, while a complete denture replaces all the teeth in the upper or lower arch.

Dentures can be useful when several teeth are missing, particularly when the gaps are spread across the mouth. They do not require implant placement, although some people choose an implant-retained denture for additional support.

Making a denture often involves records of the mouth, a trial stage, fitting and later adjustments. The gums and bone can change shape over time, so an older denture may become loose. Depending on its condition, a repair or reline may be discussed instead of making a new one.

## Checklist: what matters in your decision?

<ul style="list-style:none;padding-left:0">
<li>✅ <strong>Number and position of gaps:</strong> one missing tooth creates a different set of options from several gaps or a complete arch.</li>
<li>✅ <strong>Condition of nearby teeth:</strong> healthy intact teeth and heavily restored teeth may lead to different bridge discussions.</li>
<li>✅ <strong>Available bone:</strong> implant planning includes checking whether the jaw can support an implant.</li>
<li>✅ <strong>Fixed or removable:</strong> implants and bridges stay in the mouth, while conventional dentures are removed for cleaning.</li>
<li>✅ <strong>Surgery:</strong> implant placement is a surgical procedure; a conventional bridge or denture does not include implant placement.</li>
<li>✅ <strong>Cleaning:</strong> each option has a different cleaning routine, and being comfortable with that routine matters.</li>
<li>➖ <strong>Treatment stages:</strong> implants include a healing period, bridges involve preparing support teeth, and dentures may need try-ins and adjustments.</li>
</ul>

There is no single answer for every gap. The practical choice is the one that is clinically suitable and manageable for you day to day (cleaning is often the detail people underestimate).

## What happens at a tooth replacement assessment?

1. **The dentist examines the gap and remaining teeth.** This includes the gums, bite, existing fillings or crowns, and any denture you already use.
2. **Imaging is chosen where needed.** X-rays can show teeth and supporting structures. For implant planning, 3D CBCT imaging can provide a detailed view of the available bone.
3. **Suitable options are compared.** The discussion may include an implant, bridge, partial or complete denture, or an implant-retained restoration.
4. **The stages are explained.** You can ask what preparation, healing, fitting and maintenance each suitable option involves.
5. **You decide after the assessment.** Sometimes the examination changes the shortlist, especially when a neighbouring tooth needs treatment or the available bone differs from what was expected.
