A **dental emergency** is a problem that may need prompt assessment because of severe pain, swelling, bleeding or injury. If you are unsure, contact a dental clinic and describe what is happening. The team can help you decide between an urgent dental assessment and hospital care.

iSmile provides **same-day urgent dental care during clinic hours**, subject to appointment availability and how urgent the situation appears. Our hours are Monday to Friday, 9:30am–5:30pm, and Saturday, 9:30am–3:30pm. We do not provide a 24-hour or after-hours emergency service.

## Warning signs that may need urgent attention

Seek prompt advice from an emergency dentist if you notice one or more of these signs:

<ul style="list-style:none;padding-left:0">
  <li>✅ Severe, persistent or worsening tooth pain</li>
  <li>✅ Swelling of the gum, face or jaw</li>
  <li>✅ A knocked-out or very loose adult tooth after an injury</li>
  <li>✅ A badly broken or cracked tooth, especially if it is painful or sharp</li>
  <li>✅ Bleeding from the mouth that is difficult to control</li>
  <li>✅ An injury to the teeth, mouth, face or jaw</li>
  <li>✅ Fever or feeling unwell together with dental pain or swelling</li>
</ul>

Pain level alone does not always show how serious a problem is. A dentist needs to assess the cause before recommending treatment.

## Safe first steps while arranging dental care

These measures may help protect the area until you can be assessed. They are not a substitute for professional care.

1. **Check for medical warning signs first.** If breathing or swallowing is difficult, bleeding is heavy and will not settle, or facial or neck swelling is increasing quickly, go to a hospital emergency department.
2. **Rinse gently with clean water.** This may help clear debris after a minor mouth injury. Avoid vigorous rinsing if there is active bleeding.
3. **Apply firm pressure to bleeding.** Use clean gauze or a clean folded cloth. If the bleeding remains heavy or does not settle, seek hospital care.
4. **Use a cold compress outside the cheek.** This may help with swelling after an injury. Wrap it in a cloth and use it for short periods rather than placing ice directly on the skin.
5. **Protect a knocked-out adult tooth.** Pick it up by the crown, not the root. Do not scrub it. Keep it moist in milk if available and contact a dentist immediately. Do not force it back into place, and never replace a knocked-out baby tooth.
6. **Keep broken pieces if you can.** Place them in a clean container and bring them to the assessment.
7. **Call before travelling to the clinic.** Explain the symptoms, when they started and whether there is swelling, bleeding or trauma so the team can guide you.

## What not to do during a dental emergency

<ul style="list-style:none;padding-left:0">
  <li>❌ Do not put aspirin or other medication directly on the tooth or gum.</li>
  <li>❌ Do not apply heat to facial swelling.</li>
  <li>❌ Do not try to drain or pierce a swelling.</li>
  <li>❌ Do not use household glue on a broken tooth, filling or crown.</li>
  <li>❌ Do not poke the area with a sharp object.</li>
  <li>❌ Do not delay hospital care when breathing, swallowing, severe trauma or uncontrolled bleeding is involved.</li>
</ul>

Avoid chewing on the affected side until a dentist has assessed it. If you are considering medicine for pain, follow the product label and ask a pharmacist or doctor if you are unsure whether it is suitable for you.

## When hospital care is necessary

Some situations need medical emergency support rather than routine emergency dentistry. Go to a hospital emergency department if there is:

<ul style="list-style:none;padding-left:0">
  <li>✅ Difficulty breathing or swallowing</li>
  <li>✅ Severe facial or jaw trauma</li>
  <li>✅ Heavy bleeding that does not settle with firm pressure</li>
  <li>✅ Rapidly increasing swelling of the face or neck</li>
  <li>✅ Dental swelling together with fever, confusion, marked weakness or feeling very unwell</li>
</ul>

Outside iSmile's clinic hours, use hospital emergency services when any of these warning signs are present. For a dental problem without medical warning signs, protect the area using the steps above and contact a dental clinic when it opens.

## What to expect from an urgent dental assessment

An urgent visit starts with finding out what happened and checking for immediate risks. The exact assessment and treatment depend on the individual problem.

1. **Urgency check:** The team will ask about pain, swelling, bleeding, injury, fever, relevant health conditions and when the problem began.
2. **Dental examination:** The dentist will examine the affected tooth and surrounding tissues. An X-ray or other investigation may be recommended if it could help clarify the cause or extent of the problem.
3. **Explanation of findings:** The dentist will discuss what can be seen, what remains uncertain and the treatment options that may be suitable.
4. **Immediate care where appropriate:** Depending on the findings, care may focus on relieving the immediate problem, protecting damaged tissue or stabilising the area. Some cases need a separate follow-up visit or referral.
5. **Clear next steps:** Before you leave, the team will explain aftercare, warning signs to watch for and whether further treatment is needed.

An urgent appointment does not automatically mean a particular procedure. The aim is to assess the problem safely and agree on an appropriate plan.
