### It isn't only a children's problem

Most of what is written about mouth breathing is aimed at parents — and for good reason, since breathing affects how a child's face and jaws develop. But plenty of adults breathe through the mouth too, usually without ever having been told it matters.

If you have found yourself wondering *why am I breathing through my mouth*, or you wake most mornings with a mouth like paper, this page is for you. (If you are here about a child, our post on [mouth breathing in children](/blog/mouth-breathing-in-children) covers the growth side.)

### Why it happens

The nose is designed to do the work: it filters, warms and humidifies air before it reaches the lungs. When something makes that harder, the mouth takes over as the path of least resistance. Common reasons in adults include:

- **Nasal congestion** — allergies, chronic rhinitis or sinus inflammation
- **Structural blockage** — a deviated septum, enlarged turbinates, nasal polyps
- **Enlarged tonsils or adenoids** — less common in adults than children, but not rare
- **Long-standing habit** — the pattern set in childhood can persist even after the original blockage clears

The switch is often unconscious, and it is frequently worse at night when you have no control over it at all.

### What it does to your mouth

This is the part a dentist sees, and it is why we bother writing about it.

Saliva is the mouth's own defence system. It buffers acid, clears food debris and helps repair early enamel damage before it becomes a cavity. Breathing through an open mouth dries the whole system out — especially over seven or eight hours of sleep.

The result is a raised risk of **tooth decay and gum inflammation in someone who is doing everything right**. If you brush properly, floss, come in regularly and still keep getting cavities or persistently puffy gums, chronic dry mouth is one of the explanations worth ruling out. It is a genuinely frustrating pattern, and being told to "brush better" when you already are is not helpful.

### The signs worth taking seriously

- Waking with a very dry mouth, or a sore throat that clears through the morning
- Bad breath on waking that brushing fixes only briefly
- Snoring
- Waking unrefreshed after a full night's sleep, or daytime fatigue
- A partner who has noticed you sleep with your mouth open

That last one is often how people find out. Sleeping is not something you can observe in yourself.

### Where a dentist fits — and where we don't

We want to be straight about the boundaries here, because this is an area where it is easy to overclaim.

**What we can do:** we see the consequences early, because they appear in the mouth. At an assessment we can look at dryness, the pattern of decay, gum condition, tooth wear, tongue position and the general shape of your airway, and tell you whether what we see is consistent with mouth breathing. Dr Ling, who leads our myofunctional and airway work, sees adults as well as children for this.

**What we can't do:** we cannot treat a deviated septum or diagnose a sleep disorder. If the cause is a nasal blockage, that is an ENT specialist's job. If your snoring and exhaustion point to disturbed breathing during sleep, that belongs with a doctor or sleep physician.

Often the most useful thing a dental assessment gives you is **direction** — a clear sense of whether this is a nose problem, a sleep problem, a habit, or mainly a matter of protecting your teeth while you sort out the cause. Plenty of adults have spent years vaguely aware that they breathe through their mouth without anyone ever joining it up to their dental history.

### What happens next depends on the cause

There is no single treatment for adult mouth breathing, and anyone offering one should be treated with suspicion. Depending on what the assessment finds, the sensible next step might be a referral to have the nasal obstruction addressed, work on breathing and tongue habits, or a plan focused on protecting teeth and gums from a persistently dry mouth while the underlying problem is dealt with.

The first step is simply finding out which of those you are.
