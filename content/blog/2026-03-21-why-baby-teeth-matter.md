### The Truth About Baby Teeth

Some people call them "milk teeth." Others call them primary teeth. Whatever the name, these 20 small teeth in your child's mouth are doing important work long before they loosen and fall out.

Baby teeth typically start appearing around six months of age. By age three, most children have a full set of 20 primary teeth. These teeth then stay in place until they gradually make way for permanent teeth, usually starting around age six.

Here is why that timeline matters.

### Baby Teeth Hold Space for Adult Teeth

One of the most important jobs of baby teeth is acting as guides for the permanent teeth that follow. Each baby tooth keeps the right amount of space in the jaw for its adult replacement. If a baby tooth is lost too early due to decay or injury, the surrounding teeth can drift into that space. When the adult tooth eventually comes through, there might not be enough room, leading to crowding or misalignment.

This is why dentists often recommend protecting baby teeth with sealants or fillings rather than extracting them prematurely. Saving a baby tooth means protecting the path for its permanent successor.

### Chewing and Nutrition Start Here

Children need functional teeth to properly chew their food. Baby teeth that are decayed, infected, or missing make it uncomfortable for a child to eat a wide variety of foods. This can affect nutrition and, over time, growth development.

When a child avoids certain foods because chewing hurts, they miss out on important nutrients. Maintaining healthy baby teeth supports a varied, balanced diet during critical growth years.

### Speech Development Depends on Them

Baby teeth play a role in how children form sounds and speak clearly. The tongue, lips, and teeth all work together to produce certain sounds. When front teeth are missing or damaged, children may develop difficulty pronouncing words correctly. Addressing dental problems early helps support normal speech development.

### Baby Tooth Decay Is a Real Problem

Early childhood caries, often called baby bottle tooth decay, is one of the most common chronic childhood dental conditions. It can develop when teeth are frequently exposed to sugary liquids, including milk, formula, and fruit juices, especially through bedtime bottle feeding.

Once decay sets in, it spreads quickly through baby teeth because the enamel is thinner than on adult teeth. What starts as a small spot can become a serious infection within months. Untreated decay in baby teeth can also affect the developing adult tooth beneath.

The good news is that early childhood caries is largely preventable. Limiting sugary drinks, cleaning gums before teeth appear, and brushing twice a day with a smear of fluoride toothpaste all help.

### A Child's Confidence Grows With Their Smile

Older toddlers and preschool children become aware of their appearance. Missing or discoloured front teeth can affect a child's confidence when smiling, talking, or playing with other children. Maintaining healthy baby teeth supports your child's self-esteem during these formative social years.

### What Parents Can Do

A few straightforward habits go a long way in protecting baby teeth.

Every child is different, and what works for one may not work for another. The best step any parent can take is to bring their child to a dentist early. A pediatric or family dentist can give personalised advice based on your child's specific needs and stage of development.

Building a positive relationship with the dental clinic from a young age also helps children feel comfortable with dental visits as they grow up.

### A Foundation That Lasts

Baby teeth may be small and temporary, but they are far from insignificant. They help children eat well, speak clearly, maintain space for adult teeth, and smile with confidence. Taking care of them is one of the simplest investments in your child's long-term oral health.

Think of it this way: every healthy baby tooth that stays in place until its natural time is one less problem to solve later. Give those little teeth the attention they deserve.
